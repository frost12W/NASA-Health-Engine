PROJECT TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION HEALTH ENGINE Offline-First Astronaut
Health Monitoring Desktop Application Document type: Preliminary Project
Technical Specification (PTS) Version: 0.1 Date: 2026-11-14 Status:
Draft — pending review of the teammate’s architecture

====================================================================== 1.
PROJECT OVERVIEW
======================================================================

1.1 Product concept

Health Engine is an offline-first desktop application intended to
support routine health-status checks and health-data review for a crew
of astronauts. It uses a connected or built-in camera as an input source
for experimental camera-based physiological signal analysis (rPPG),
stores records locally, presents crew status in a clear operational
interface, reminds crew members to complete scheduled checks, and
synchronizes selected records with a ground-side system when a
communication link is available.

The product is a monitoring and screening support tool. It is NOT a
medical diagnostic system, does not replace certified medical equipment,
and must not independently determine fitness for duty or prescribe
treatment.

1.2 Core product principles

-   Local-first operation: essential application functions remain
    available without an internet or satellite connection.
-   Explicit data provenance: distinguish measured, self-reported,
    calculated, imported, simulated, and unavailable values.
-   Honest signal quality: do not display an estimated vital sign as
    reliable when the source signal is insufficient.
-   Human oversight: abnormal indicators create a review task for crew
    or authorized medical personnel; they do not constitute a diagnosis.
-   Resilient synchronization: data remains stored locally until
    successful, acknowledged transfer.
-   Minimal operational UI: prioritize status, pending checks, alerts,
    and understandable explanations.
-   Safety over gamification: game mechanics must never shame, coerce,
    conceal, or override health-related warnings.

1.3 Target environment

Initial target: a desktop computer on a spacecraft or in a simulated
mission environment, with a camera, local storage, display, and optional
network or satellite communication gateway.

The application can only collect camera data while the computer,
application, camera, and required permissions are operational. “24/7
monitoring” must not be claimed unless continuous capture, power, camera
availability, and background execution have been implemented and
validated. The initial MVP should describe itself as scheduled or
user-initiated monitoring.

======================================================================
2. GOALS AND NON-GOALS
======================================================================

2.1 Goals

-   Provide a crew overview with the latest available health-check
    status.
-   Guide an astronaut through a short, repeatable health-check
    workflow.
-   Acquire camera frames and attempt rPPG signal extraction.
-   Show a pulse estimate only when signal-quality criteria are met.
-   Record sleep and mood information where required by the selected
    workflow.
-   Provide understandable explanations of metrics, limitations, and
    risk flags.
-   Remind crew members about scheduled checks.
-   Preserve records locally in SQLite and organize monthly
    exports/archives.
-   Synchronize approved records when a communication channel is
    available.
-   Export selected records in PDF, JSON, and TXT formats.
-   Provide a simple, operationally focused interface.

2.2 Non-goals for the initial release

-   Medical diagnosis, treatment recommendations, or fitness-for-duty
    decisions.
-   Replacing ECG, pulse oximetry, blood pressure monitors, or other
    certified medical instruments.
-   Guaranteed measurement accuracy across all cameras, skin tones,
    lighting, motion conditions, or environments.
-   Guaranteed access to undocumented camera controls or hardware
    settings.
-   Streaming raw video to Earth by default.
-   Implementing a real satellite modem or spacecraft communications
    protocol without a defined hardware/interface specification.
-   Automatic deletion of historical health records.
-   Unsupervised machine-learning diagnosis.

======================================================================
3. USERS AND ROLES
======================================================================

3.1 Astronaut / Crew member

-   Views their own profile and assigned check tasks.
-   Completes scheduled measurements and self-reported entries.
-   Reads explanations and instructions.
-   Sees their own historical measurements and status, subject to
    mission policy.

3.2 Crew medical officer / authorized reviewer

-   Reviews crew overview and flagged records.
-   Opens detailed astronaut profiles.
-   Reviews measurement quality, trends, and data provenance.
-   Acknowledges and documents alert review where permitted.

3.3 Mission administrator

-   Manages crew profiles and mission configuration.
-   Configures mission start time, circadian schedule, reminder policy,
    and synchronization endpoint through an authorized configuration
    process.
-   Reviews synchronization status and storage warnings.
-   Controls access and export permissions.

Role-based access may be simplified for the hackathon prototype, but the
data model should not assume that every user has unrestricted access to
every crew member’s detailed health data.

4. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
======================================================================

FR-001. Crew overview The HOME screen shall display all configured
astronaut profiles, each with name or crew identifier, latest check
timestamp, completion state, and a clear status indicator.

FR-002. Profile detail Selecting a crew profile shall open a detailed
page containing the latest available measurements, measurement
timestamps, signal quality, recent history, sleep/mood entries where
enabled, assigned tasks, and explanations.

FR-003. Camera discovery The application shall enumerate cameras
available to the operating system and allow selection of an available
camera. It shall show permission, connection, and device errors clearly.

FR-004. Camera configuration The acquisition component shall request
supported camera modes and select the best validated mode available for
the device. It may attempt to request YUY2 or another uncompressed
format when supported.

The application must not assume that it can force a camera to provide
uncompressed frames, disable all compression, set “quality to 100%”, or
control every driver-level setting. These controls depend on camera
hardware, driver, operating system, and capture API. The actual
negotiated format, resolution, frame rate, and pixel format must be
recorded and displayed.

FR-005. Video acquisition The application shall acquire a sequence of
timestamped frames for a measurement session. A still image alone is
insufficient for rPPG.

FR-006. rPPG processing The Python computer-vision component shall: -
Receive frames or a supported local video stream. - Detect and track a
face. - Select suitable skin regions of interest (ROI), such as forehead
and cheeks. - Extract per-frame color statistics. - Build a time-series
signal. - Apply an appropriate rPPG method and signal filtering. -
Estimate pulse rate only when quality checks pass. - Return the
estimate, confidence/quality indicators, processing metadata, and
failure reason where applicable.

FR-007. Measurement quality The system shall evaluate at least the
availability of sufficient frames, effective frame rate, signal
stability, motion/artifact level, and usable signal duration.
Low-quality measurements shall be marked invalid or inconclusive rather
than converted into a misleading numeric result.

FR-008. Measurement result A measurement record shall include astronaut
ID, start/end timestamps, metric value where valid, unit,
method/version, source device, signal-quality metadata, validity state,
and any applicable limitations.

FR-009. Daily check reminders The application shall show a due/pending
reminder for each astronaut based on the configured schedule. Reminder
completion must be recorded.

FR-010. Tasks and gamification The astronaut profile may show: - One
mandatory health-check task. - One or two additional approved wellness
or operational tasks. - Completion state and achievement-style visual
feedback.

Task count and presentation may adapt to approved status rules.
Gamification must not encourage unsafe exercise, conceal a warning,
penalize an astronaut for an abnormal result, or incentivize repeated
measurements until a desired value appears.

FR-011. Status categories The UI may display categories such as GOOD,
MODERATE, LOW, and REVIEW REQUIRED only when their definitions and
thresholds have been approved by the project/domain reviewers. The
categories must be described as screening or workflow indicators, not
diagnoses. Missing or low-quality data must be shown as UNKNOWN /
INSUFFICIENT DATA, not as GOOD.

FR-012. Alert handling A significant configured warning shall be visible
on the crew overview and in the relevant astronaut profile. An audible
alert may be used for high-priority events, subject to a safe and
configurable alert policy.

Audible alerts must: - Have a defined severity and escalation policy. -
Be acknowledgeable by an authorized user. - Avoid an unbounded or
endlessly repeating audio loop. - Continue to show a persistent visual
alert after audio is acknowledged. - Record creation, acknowledgement,
and resolution timestamps. - Avoid exposing sensitive details through
sound or public notifications.

The original concept of playing audio continuously until someone opens
the profile is not recommended. Opening a profile is not equivalent to
reviewing or resolving an alert. Use explicit acknowledgement and
escalation instead.

FR-013. Health Engine Wiki The application shall provide concise help
pages explaining: - What each displayed metric means. - How camera-based
pulse estimation works at a high level. - What affects measurement
quality. - What each status category means. - What the application can
and cannot conclude. - How to complete required checks. - How reminders,
tasks, alerts, and synchronization work.

FR-014. Local storage All measurement and journal records shall be saved
locally in SQLite. Records shall not be silently deleted by normal
application operation.

FR-015. Monthly organization The application shall organize monthly
health-result exports or archive packages under a directory named:

ASTRONAUTS-HEALTH-RESULTS-MONTH/

A recommended structure is:

HealthEngine/ database/ health_engine.sqlite
ASTRONAUTS-HEALTH-RESULTS-MONTH/ YYYY-MM/ crew-summary.json
measurements.json audit-log.json report.pdf exports/ logs/ backups/

The SQLite database remains the primary local source of truth. Monthly
files are generated snapshots/exports and must not be treated as the
only copy. The actual application data directory should use the
operating system’s approved per-user or application-data location. The
README shall explain where this directory is located on each supported
operating system.

FR-016. Storage monitoring The application shall monitor available disk
space and the approximate size of its own data directory. It shall warn
before storage becomes critically low. It shall not automatically delete
health records. It may offer an authorized backup/export workflow.

FR-017. Synchronization The application shall maintain an outbound queue
for approved records. Each queued item shall have a stable unique
identifier, creation timestamp, payload version, status, retry metadata,
and acknowledgement state.

Synchronization shall support: - Offline queuing. - Retry with
backoff. - Batch transfer. - Acknowledgement from the receiving
endpoint. - Idempotent processing to prevent duplicate records. -
Integrity checks. - Encryption in transit. - Clear display of last
successful sync and pending item count.

FR-018. Data minimization Raw video shall be processed locally and not
transmitted by default. The default outbound payload should contain only
required derived measurements and metadata. Any raw-video retention or
transfer requires a separately approved use case, consent/policy, access
control, and storage plan.

FR-019. Exports Authorized users shall be able to export selected
records in PDF, JSON, and TXT formats. Export operations shall indicate
the selected date range, astronaut, generation time, and whether values
are measured, self-reported, or calculated.

FR-020. Settings The initial Settings page shall provide read-only
application version and export-format selection, plus necessary
operational information such as storage and synchronization status.
Settings must not expose unsafe hardware or mission-critical controls
without authorization.

5. USER INTERFACE AND NAVIGATION
======================================================================

5.1 Visual direction

The interface should use a restrained aerospace/mission-control visual
language inspired by the clarity and information hierarchy of Warframe’s
interface, without copying its visual assets or sacrificing usability.

Requirements: - Fast access to the crew overview. - Clear typography and
strong information hierarchy. - Monospace typography for mission time
and telemetry-style values. - Consistent status colors with text labels;
color must not be the only communication method. - Avoid decorative
animation that competes with health alerts. - Support the target display
resolution and scaling.

5.2 Main layout

A. Top mission status bar

Display four time/context fields:

1.  UTC (Zulu Time) Format example: 2026-11-14 | 18:58:00.4 UTC Source:
    system clock normalized to UTC. Precision shown must not imply
    measurement accuracy beyond the system clock’s actual
    synchronization quality.

2.  DOY (Day of Year) Format example: DOY 318 Derived from the UTC date
    using the selected calendar convention.

3.  MET (Mission Elapsed Time) Format example: MET +042d 14:20:05
    Derived from the configured mission epoch/start timestamp. Mission
    start time must be configured, not hardcoded as a universal value.

4.  Crew Circadian Phase Example: 06:00–21:30 UTC / DAY SHIFT Example:
    21:30–06:00 UTC / SLEEP CYCLE The schedule is a mission
    configuration. It should not be assumed that every astronaut shares
    the same sleep schedule.

All four fields shall use a monospace font. The UI should indicate when
the system clock is not synchronized or its accuracy is unknown.

B. Left navigation

1.  HOME Crew overview, mission time bar, reminders, alerts, and
    synchronization status.

2.  HEALTH ACHIEVE Achievement-style task view, completion history, and
    approved wellness challenges. Avoid competitive rankings unless
    explicitly approved by mission policy.

3.  DETAIL MONITORING Crew cards and detailed astronaut profiles.

4.  HEALTH ENGINE WIKI Concise explanations, instructions, metric
    definitions, and limitations. A NASA-inspired book icon may be used
    only as a visual motif; do not imply NASA endorsement or official
    affiliation.

5.  SETTINGS Application version, export formats, storage status, and
    permitted operational settings.

5.3 HOME screen content

-   Crew member cards.
-   Latest check state and timestamp.
-   Pending mandatory checks.
-   High-priority alerts.
-   Last successful synchronization.
-   Pending synchronization count.
-   Persistent notice explaining the actual monitoring scope and
    reporting schedule.

The notice must not claim 24/7 collection unless the feature is
genuinely running continuously. If checks are scheduled, use wording
such as: “Health checks are recorded when completed. Approved summaries
are prepared for periodic reporting.”

======================================================================
6. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE
======================================================================

6.1 Proposed technology stack

Desktop shell and system integration: - Tauri. - Rust for application
lifecycle, filesystem access, local database coordination, scheduling,
synchronization queue, and controlled hardware integration.

Frontend: - React. - TypeScript. - A component-based UI with accessible
status indicators and charts.

Computer vision and signal processing: - Python. - OpenCV for
camera/frame handling and image processing. - Appropriate scientific
signal-processing libraries where justified, such as NumPy and SciPy. -
A defined local IPC interface between the Rust/Tauri host and Python
processing service.

Persistence: - SQLite as the local primary database. - Versioned
database migrations. - Transactional writes and backup strategy.

Communication: - A transport abstraction for local testing and future
network/satellite integration. - HTTPS or a mission-approved transport
protocol when connected. - Do not assume that a satellite modem exposes
ordinary internet access.

6.2 Component responsibilities

Tauri/Rust: - Start and supervise the desktop application. - Manage
application configuration and lifecycle. - Provide controlled filesystem
access. - Maintain local persistence and database transactions, or
coordinate a dedicated database layer. - Run reminders and storage
checks while the application is running. - Maintain the synchronization
queue. - Perform secure transport and acknowledgement handling. - Manage
application logs and update verification. - Expose a narrow, validated
command interface to the frontend.

React/TypeScript: - Render navigation, crew cards, profiles, charts,
tasks, alerts, and Wiki. - Present camera status and measurement
workflow. - Display quality and provenance metadata. - Avoid
implementing security-sensitive filesystem or transport operations
directly in the frontend.

Python/OpenCV: - Discover or open a selected camera through supported OS
APIs. - Negotiate supported capture modes. - Acquire timestamped
frames. - Detect/track face and skin ROIs. - Extract signals and
estimate pulse where valid. - Return structured results and
diagnostics. - Release camera resources safely when a session ends.

6.3 Camera and video pipeline

Proposed processing path:

Camera device -> OS capture API / camera driver -> negotiated pixel
format and frame rate -> Python frame acquisition -> frame timestamps ->
face detection and tracking -> skin ROI selection -> color signal
extraction -> rPPG algorithm -> filtering and artifact/quality
assessment -> pulse estimate + quality metadata -> Rust application
service -> SQLite -> React UI

Important hardware limitation: Tauri and Rust do not automatically
provide unrestricted control over camera compression, pixel format,
exposure, gain, focus, or image quality. The available controls depend
on the operating system, driver, camera firmware, and capture backend.
YUY2 is a possible negotiated format on some devices, not a guaranteed
format. “Uncompressed” does not necessarily mean more accurate rPPG;
frame rate, exposure stability, lighting, motion, and signal quality
also matter.

The implementation shall query available modes, request a supported
mode, verify the negotiated result, and record the actual mode. It shall
not silently claim YUY2 or 100% quality if the device did not provide
it.

6.4 Local process communication

The Python worker may run as a supervised child process. Communication
options include standard input/output using versioned JSON messages or a
local IPC mechanism. The selected method must: - Validate message schema
and size. - Include request/session IDs. - Handle worker crashes and
timeouts. - Avoid sending unnecessary raw frame data through the UI
layer. - Provide explicit start, stop, and health-check operations. -
Ensure camera resources are released after failure or shutdown.

6.5 Database model (initial entities)

Astronaut: - astronaut_id - crew_identifier - display_name - role (if
applicable) - profile_status - created_at - updated_at

MeasurementSession: - session_id - astronaut_id - started_at_utc -
ended_at_utc - camera_device_id - capture_format - resolution -
effective_fps - algorithm_name - algorithm_version - session_status

MetricRecord: - metric_id - session_id - metric_type - value - unit -
validity_status - quality_score or quality metadata - source_type -
measured_at_utc

JournalEntry: - entry_id - astronaut_id - entry_type (sleep, mood, or
approved type) - value - unit_or_scale - recorded_at_utc - source_type

Task: - task_id - astronaut_id - task_type - due_at_utc - status -
completed_at_utc

Alert: - alert_id - astronaut_id - severity - rule_id - created_at_utc -
acknowledged_at_utc - acknowledged_by - resolved_at_utc - status

SyncQueueItem: - item_id - record_type - record_id - payload_version -
created_at_utc - status - retry_count - next_retry_at_utc -
acknowledged_at_utc

AuditEvent: - event_id - actor_id - event_type - target_type -
target_id - timestamp_utc - details

Thresholds, risk rules, and status mappings must be versioned and
traceable to approved sources. Do not embed invented clinical thresholds
in code.

======================================================================
7. HEALTH STATUS, RISK AND GAMIFICATION RULES
======================================================================

7.1 Status model

The initial system may use a simple status model: - GOOD: no configured
screening rule currently requires review, and required data is
sufficiently recent and valid. - MODERATE: a non-urgent trend or
incomplete task requires attention. - LOW: a configured rule indicates a
notable deviation requiring review. - REVIEW REQUIRED: a high-priority
configured rule requires timely human review. - UNKNOWN: data is
missing, stale, invalid, or of insufficient quality.

These are workflow labels, not medical diagnoses. Exact definitions and
thresholds require domain review and source documentation.

7.2 Task adaptation

The number and type of tasks may be adapted only using approved rules.
An abnormal measurement must not automatically prescribe exercise or
increase physical workload. Additional tasks should be safe, optional
where appropriate, and approved for the mission context.

7.3 Achievement presentation

-   Pending task: visually distinct, e.g. amber achievement-style icon.
-   Completed task: green completion state.
-   Overdue task: clear text and a separate overdue marker.
-   Alert state: distinct from achievement state.
-   Unknown data: neutral state, never green by default.

The UI should reward completion of the check process, not a favorable
health result. Astronauts must not be incentivized to manipulate or
repeat readings to obtain a better score.

======================================================================
8. ALERTS AND NOTIFICATIONS
======================================================================

Alerts shall be based on versioned rules and valid data. The system
shall distinguish: - Measurement-quality warning. - Routine task
reminder. - Trend requiring review. - High-priority health screening
flag. - System fault (camera, storage, clock, database, sync).

For audio: - Use a short, recognizable alert sound for approved
high-priority events. - Provide a clear acknowledge action. - Keep a
persistent visual alert until reviewed/resolved according to policy. -
Apply a bounded repeat interval and maximum repeat policy. - Provide
mute/silence controls only to authorized roles and preserve visual
escalation. - Log every alert state transition.

Do not use an endless sound loop or require opening a profile as the
only way to silence audio. Alert acknowledgement and medical resolution
are separate actions.

======================================================================
9. OFFLINE OPERATION AND SYNCHRONIZATION
======================================================================

9.1 Offline behavior

Without external connectivity, the application shall still: - Open the
local UI. - Read local crew profiles and records. - Perform supported
camera measurements. - Run local signal processing. - Save records to
SQLite. - Display reminders while the application is running. - Display
locally generated alerts. - Generate local exports. - Queue records for
later synchronization.

The application cannot guarantee collection when the computer is powered
off, the app is not running, the camera is disconnected, or the
operating system suspends the process.

9.2 Synchronization lifecycle

Local record created -> queued as PENDING -> connection available ->
batch prepared -> payload encrypted and sent -> receiver validates and
stores -> receiver returns acknowledgement -> local item marked SYNCED

On failure: - Retain the local record. - Record the failure reason. -
Retry with bounded exponential backoff. - Prevent duplicate remote
records through stable IDs/idempotency keys. - Do not mark data as
synchronized before acknowledgement.

9.3 Transport abstraction

Define a transport interface so that the application can use a
test/local transport, ordinary IP network, or a future mission-specific
satellite gateway. The satellite implementation is outside the MVP until
its hardware, protocol, authentication, bandwidth, and operational
constraints are known.

9.4 Data prioritization

Suggested priority: 1. Approved high-priority alert metadata. 2. Latest
measurement summaries and required health records. 3. Routine task and
journal records. 4. Aggregated historical summaries. 5. Optional
diagnostics.

Raw video is excluded by default.

======================================================================
10. STORAGE, BACKUP AND RETENTION
======================================================================

-   SQLite is the primary local record store.
-   Records are retained by default; no automatic deletion.
-   Monthly export packages are placed under
    ASTRONAUTS-HEALTH-RESULTS-MONTH/YYYY-MM/.
-   Monitor both application data size and free disk space.
-   Warn at configurable thresholds before storage exhaustion.
-   Provide authorized backup/export operations.
-   Verify backups and record their creation time.
-   Protect database files and exports using OS permissions and
    encryption where supported and required by the mission security
    policy.
-   Do not store secrets or credentials in plaintext configuration
    files.

The project README must document the exact data directory for each
supported OS, database location, monthly archive structure, backup
process, and retention behavior.

======================================================================
11. SECURITY AND PRIVACY
======================================================================

-   Restrict access to crew health information by role and mission
    policy.
-   Keep camera processing local by default.
-   Do not transmit raw video unless explicitly authorized.
-   Encrypt network transfers.
-   Protect local files and backups according to the target OS
    capabilities.
-   Validate all IPC messages and frontend-to-Rust commands.
-   Apply least privilege to the Python worker.
-   Avoid logging raw health data or sensitive personal details
    unnecessarily.
-   Maintain an audit trail for exports, acknowledgements, and
    synchronization.
-   Use signed application updates and verify update integrity.
-   Define key management and recovery before operational deployment.

======================================================================
12. TIME AND CLOCK HANDLING
======================================================================

-   Store timestamps in UTC using a consistent machine-readable
    representation.
-   Display UTC in the mission header.
-   Derive DOY from the UTC date.
-   Derive MET from a configured mission epoch.
-   Derive circadian phase from an approved schedule and the correct
    time basis.
-   Record clock synchronization status and uncertainty where available.
-   Do not treat display precision as proof of clock accuracy.
-   Use monotonic clocks for elapsed intervals within a running process
    where appropriate; use UTC timestamps for persistent event records.

The example date/time and MET values in the concept are examples only.
Mission epoch and schedules must be configured for the actual mission.

======================================================================
13. UPDATES AND APPLICATION LIFECYCLE
======================================================================

-   Essential offline functionality must not depend on update
    availability.
-   Updates must be signed and integrity-checked.
-   Updates should be staged and installed only under an approved
    policy.
-   Support rollback to a known-good version where technically feasible.
-   Apply database migrations safely and back up data before
    incompatible migrations.
-   Display current application version.
-   Do not force an update during an active measurement or critical
    operation.
-   Document supported OS versions and hardware requirements.

======================================================================
14. FAILURE MODES AND RECOVERY
======================================================================

Camera unavailable: - Show device error. - Allow device reselection. -
Do not create a valid measurement record.

Unsupported capture format: - Fall back to a supported mode. - Record
the actual negotiated format. - Show a diagnostic message.

Insufficient rPPG signal: - Mark the result inconclusive. - Explain
likely causes such as motion, lighting, or short capture duration. -
Offer a new measurement only where appropriate.

Python worker crash: - Stop the measurement safely. - Release camera
resources if possible. - Record a technical error. - Restart the worker
only under controlled recovery logic.

Database write failure: - Do not report the measurement as saved. -
Preserve recoverable data in a controlled temporary buffer where
feasible. - Show a prominent storage/database warning.

Disk space low: - Warn the user. - Stop nonessential exports or cache
growth if necessary. - Never silently delete health records.

Network/satellite unavailable: - Continue local operation. - Retain
queued records. - Display pending synchronization state.

Clock not synchronized: - Continue local operation where safe. - Flag
timestamp uncertainty. - Avoid misleading mission-time interpretation.

Alert audio unavailable: - Keep a persistent visual alert. - Record the
audio subsystem failure.

15. MVP SCOPE AND IMPLEMENTATION PRIORITIES
======================================================================

P0 — Demonstrable core - Tauri desktop shell and React/TypeScript UI. -
Python/OpenCV camera acquisition. - Camera discovery and actual
capture-mode reporting. - Basic face/ROI extraction. - rPPG proof of
concept with quality gating. - One astronaut profile and a small crew
list. - SQLite persistence. - UTC/DOY/MET/circadian header using
configurable mission settings. - Local dashboard and detailed profile. -
Manual measurement workflow. - Clear signal-quality and limitation
messages.

P1 — Operational workflow - Multiple astronaut profiles. - Daily check
reminders. - Task completion and achievement-style states. - Sleep/mood
journal if included in approved scope. - Versioned screening rules and
status indicators. - Wiki/help section. - Local alerts with
acknowledgement. - JSON/TXT/PDF exports. - Storage monitoring and
monthly archive generation.

P2 — Resilience and mission integration - Offline sync queue and
simulated connection recovery. - Idempotent remote transfer protocol. -
Satellite transport adapter once interface details exist. - Signed
update workflow. - Backup/restore validation. - Role-based access and
audit trail. - Long-duration reliability and performance tests.

The first technical gate is a real-camera rPPG proof of concept. Do not
build the full UI and gamification system before proving that the
selected camera, capture mode, and algorithm can produce a sufficiently
stable signal under representative conditions.

======================================================================
16. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
======================================================================

A build may be considered an MVP candidate when:

AC-001. The desktop application launches without an internet connection.
AC-002. It can enumerate and open at least one supported camera. AC-003.
It reports the actual negotiated pixel format, resolution, and FPS.
AC-004. It can capture a timestamped frame sequence. AC-005. It can
produce an rPPG estimate on a validated test setup, or clearly report an
inconclusive result when quality is insufficient. AC-006. It never
presents an invalid estimate as a valid measurement. AC-007. It stores
measurement metadata and results in SQLite. AC-008. A saved record
remains available after application restart. AC-009. The crew overview
and astronaut profile update from stored records. AC-010. UTC, DOY, MET,
and circadian phase are displayed from configured sources and settings.
AC-011. A due task is visible and completion is persisted. AC-012.
Alerts can be acknowledged and their state transitions are logged.
AC-013. The application does not delete health records automatically.
AC-014. Low disk space produces a visible warning. AC-015. Export files
contain timestamps, units, provenance, and validity. AC-016. With
connectivity disabled, local workflows continue to operate. AC-017.
Queued records are not marked synchronized until acknowledged. AC-018.
The UI explains that rPPG is an estimate and not a medical diagnosis.

18. RISKS AND TECHNICAL VALIDATION GATES
======================================================================

RISK-01: rPPG accuracy and reliability Mitigation: early proof of
concept, representative camera testing, quality gating, comparison
against a reference device during development, and transparent
limitations.

RISK-02: Camera format/control assumptions Mitigation: enumerate and
negotiate supported modes; never assume YUY2, uncompressed output, fixed
exposure, or “100% quality” is available.

RISK-03: Medical overclaim Mitigation: use screening/support language,
source all rules, and require human review. Do not claim diagnostic
accuracy.

RISK-04: False or missed alerts Mitigation: conservative, validated
rules; quality checks; alert audit trail; human oversight; no single
unvalidated metric as a diagnosis.

RISK-05: Offline data loss Mitigation: transactional SQLite writes,
backup/restore tests, durable sync queue, and explicit acknowledgement.

RISK-06: Scope overload Mitigation: prove camera/rPPG first; implement
the desktop shell and basic local data flow before gamification,
satellite integration, and advanced reporting.

RISK-07: Continuous-monitoring overclaim Mitigation: clearly state
whether monitoring is scheduled, user-initiated, or continuous. Test
background operation, power behavior, camera availability, and recovery
before claiming 24/7 coverage.
